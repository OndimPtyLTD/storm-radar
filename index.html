      Thunderwatch

      Thunderwatch South Africa is a free weather and storm information service for anyone in South Africa. It shows estimated thunderstorm risk, rain and wind in 30-minute steps for the next 24 hours, compares six forecast sources, and shows recent rain radar where coverage exists.

      It is free to use, needs no account, and will stay that way. It was developed by Mbasa Faku and is run by Ondim (Pty) Ltd.

      Forecasts

      Forecast data comes from Open-Meteo, which serves several global weather models (ECMWF, GFS, ICON and the UK Met Office), and from MET Norway. Thunderwatch checks a grid of points about 300 km across around the place you choose.

      Thunderstorm risk

      The risk categories (Low, Moderate, High, Very high, Extreme) are estimates built from forecast atmospheric instability (CAPE and lifted index), rain chance and the models’ own thunderstorm codes. They are not calibrated probabilities and they are not observed storms.

      Storm movement

      Arrows show where storms would drift, based on the average wind between about 1.5 and 5.5 km up. Arrival times in the summary are model estimates, not tracked storms.

      South Africa view and alerts

      The South Africa view checks about 150 points roughly 110 km apart across the country, and the free storm alerts use the same grid every 30 minutes for 33 towns in all nine provinces. Because the grid is coarse, alerts describe the area around a town, not a single street. To limit false alarms, alerts go out only for Extreme risk, or Very high risk where a model also forecasts thunder.

      Radar

      The radar view shows observed rain from RainViewer for the last two hours. Coverage over South Africa has gaps, and an empty area on the radar is not proof of dry weather.

      Limitations

      Thunderstorms are small and fast-changing, and global models cannot place them exactly. Treat Thunderwatch as an early read, and use official warnings for decisions about safety.

      If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck by lightning. Go indoors or into a hard-topped vehicle and stay there until 30 minutes after the last thunder.

      • Stay away from open fields, hilltops, isolated trees, water and metal fences during a storm.
      • Never drive or walk through flowing floodwater; a small depth of fast water can sweep a car away.
      • Unplug sensitive electronics before a storm arrives, not during it.
      • Follow warnings from the South African Weather Service and your local disaster management centre.

      Forecasts: Open-Meteo (ECMWF, GFS, ICON and UK Met Office models) and MET Norway Locationforecast. Rain radar: RainViewer. Base map: Esri, with OpenStreetMap contributors. Place search: Open-Meteo geocoding.

      Thunderwatch supplements, and does not replace, official severe-weather warnings. For official warnings, see the South African Weather Service at weathersa.co.za.

      Forecasts can be wrong. Thunderwatch gives no guarantee of accuracy and is not liable for decisions made using it.

      Thunderwatch has no accounts, sets no cookies and does not sell or share personal information.

      Location

      If you tap the location button, your browser asks permission first. Your approximate coordinates are then sent to the weather services listed under Data sources to fetch your forecast. Thunderwatch does not store them. Place names you type are sent to Open-Meteo’s place search.

      Places you save are kept only in this browser on your device. Storm alert subscriptions live in the ntfy app; Thunderwatch never sees who subscribes.

      Visitor statistics

      Thunderwatch uses GoatCounter, a cookieless visitor counter, to see how many people use the site, roughly which country or province they visit from, and which features are used. It does not identify individuals.
